## The family this round fixed: a spoken unit keeps its letter

A dump of all 56 failures grouped them:

- **22** the unit family (8 misspelled + 7 canonical + 7 spoken) — the largest;
- 17 a miss (no answer at all) — 6 misspelled, 6 spoken, 5 canonical;
- 12 a wrong road or suburb — 7 on the unknown-number ask, 3 with the road type
  omitted, 2 misspelled;
- 5 spread over the rest.

The unit family's root cause was two lines that disagreed with the register:

1. `nz_address.parse` split a SPOKEN unit that carries a letter — "unit 287C 28" became
   unit 287 with suffix C — even though the register's row is `287C/28` (unit `287C`,
   number `28`). The invented suffix then matched the PLAIN `28C` row that exists
   beside the unit row.
2. `_prefer_plain_address` compared the driver's unit to the register's case-
   sensitively (`"287C" != "287c"`), so even a correctly parsed unit "matched" nothing
   and the selector returned the first candidate by store order — a coin flip between
   the plain row and the unit row.

The fix: a spoken unit keeps its letter (exactly as a unit written before a slash
already did), and the unit comparison folds the case. The red-first tests are in
`services/api/tests/test_unit_addresses.py` (the spoken lettered unit, the
letter-only unit, the register's case, and the two-row end-to-end where the plain row
sits beside the unit row); the stale test that pinned the split is rewritten with the
register's evidence in its docstring.
